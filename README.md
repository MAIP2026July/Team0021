### Title
#### SkillLens: An Adaptive Competency Diagnostic and Precision Remediation Engine

### Industry / Function
#### Enterprise Learning & Development (L&D) / Engineering & Business Operations / Talent Acquisition

### Target User
Primary: Enterprise employees across technical (e.g., software engineering, data, cloud) and business tracks (e.g., product management, operations) seeking capability benchmarking and precision upskilling.

Secondary: Functional People Managers, L&D Specialists, and Technical Hiring Panels evaluating role-fit and approving remediation pathways.

### Current Workflow Problem
Workforce capability evaluations currently depend on multiple-choice quizzes that reward memorization over applied reasoning, or on subjective, uncalibrated manager check-ins. As a result, employees are assigned generic, 40-hour course catalogs that suffer from low completion rates and fail to close verified skill gaps. Furthermore, hiring pipelines face identical friction: initial candidate screens rely on easily gamified multiple-choice tests or exhaust senior engineering and business leads with repetitive, manual 45-minute screening interviews.

### Why This Matters
Enterprises spend heavily on digital learning subscriptions and recruiting operations, yet persistent skill deficits delay critical product roadmaps. Misdiagnosing internal competencies results in wasted training hours and misallocated team resources, while uncalibrated pre-hire assessments cause high screening drop-offs or expensive mis-hires. Automating scenario-based evaluation and targeted remediation cuts hundreds of hours in management overhead and shortens time-to-productivity.

### Proposed AI Role
1. Dynamic, role-conditioned scenario generation (architectural tradeoffs for technical tracks; operational escalations for business tracks).
2. Semantic evaluation and rubric-grounded scoring of open-ended situational submissions.
3. Confidence-scored gap diagnosis paired with verbatim evidence citations.
4. Precision matching and retrieval of granular learning assets (e.g., specific 15-minute lessons rather than full 40-hour courses).
